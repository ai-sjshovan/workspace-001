# Core Link Protagonist And Family

This document captures the current first-draft protagonist, family, and close
home-life direction for Core Link. Names are intentionally open.

## Protagonist Draft

The protagonist is an older teen, roughly 16-18. He is male and lives in a
tropical island metro district where scavenging, repair, debt, corruption, and
status pressure shape daily life.

Home city inspiration:

- **Relay City** is the wider tropical island metro.
- **Quezar City / QZ** is the protagonist's home district, named after Arturo
  Quezar and later absorbed into corpo recovery-zone branding.
- **Kubo Junction** is the local transit/market hub: rail lines, bus terminals,
  malls, repair stalls, back alleys, overpasses, traffic, storms, black-market
  parts, scavenger routes, and street commerce.
- A subtle Rochester Hills, Michigan naming/personal-memory influence can be
  folded into district, road, school, park, hill, or old subdivision names, but
  the main visual identity should remain tropical Philippine metro.

Visual direction:

- dark hair
- slightly longer, practical hair
- possible short goatee or early facial hair
- almost steampunk-like repair goggles
- baggy pants
- sturdy shoes
- hoodie or loose outer layer despite the heat
- gloves
- no beanie for now unless a later design needs it

The clothing should make practical sense. He is a scavenger working around
sharp scrap, corroded metal, broken plastic, wires, rain, heat, and dirty repair
jobs. Baggy protective clothing and gloves help prevent cuts, burns, grime, and
scrapes even if the climate is hot.

## Skills And Role

He is a scavenger and repair-minded tech kid rather than a chosen hero. He has a
natural talent for fixing broken devices, reading scrap value, improvising
tools, and understanding old ARCs/Kin enough to get more out of them than most
people expect.

He has a few close scavenging/trading friends, but the relationships are shaped
by survival more than comfort. They rely on each other, trade with each other,
and watch each other's backs, but life has made emotional closeness hard.

## Values

He is on the side of ordinary people against corpos.

He is angry at the coldness of the world: people walk past suffering, corpos
hide behind paperwork, and money decides whose pain matters. He still tries to
help when he can, even when he has little. If someone is begging or struggling,
he may give a small amount of food, parts, money, or attention, even when it
costs him.

He holds high moral and ethical standards for himself and his family. He is not
perfect and can forget, lash out, or act from frustration, but he is trying to
be good in a world that rewards selfishness.

## Faith

The family is religious and prays to God daily, including before meals. The
protagonist takes this seriously. Faith should be part of the family's rhythm
and moral center, not a superficial label.

He may forget to pray sometimes or struggle with anger, grief, and doubt, but he
still believes his choices matter and that he is accountable for how he treats
people.

## Family

The protagonist does everything he can to provide for his mother and little
sister.

### Father

His father is dead. He was killed in a horrible workplace accident while working
for a corpo. The corporation avoided compensating the family by finding or
creating a loophole that blamed the father for the accident.

The surface story is a workplace accident. The deeper truth is that CoreTech
staged the accident after exposing him.

The father worked for CoreTech despite the suffering the company caused because
the family needed income and because his work touched something he believed
could still protect people. CoreTech did not pay him well or treat him well, and
this created tension: he was employed by the same system hurting his community.

His gift was deciphering. He could read patterns, broken keys, corrupted
packets, strange ARC behavior, and K.I.N. security anomalies in a way few people
could. CoreTech used that gift to help keep portions of the K.I.N. network
secure and operational for public-facing systems, licensed Kin, services, and
infrastructure.

Secretly, he was also working with underground factions from inside CoreTech.
Using his access to K.I.N. routes, corpo PCs, security tools, and internal
records, he helped expose vulnerabilities, protect communities, pass intelligence,
and slowly dismantle parts of the company from within. He operated under a
network codename that should be decided later.

His motives should stay morally complicated. He did not begin as a flawless
martyr. At first, he leaked or sold CoreTech secrets because the family was
under pressure and CoreTech was underpaying and exploiting him. He told himself
the company deserved it and that the money was for survival. Early leaks may
have seemed harmless: old routing maps, buried safety reports, abandoned K.I.N.
nodes, scrap shipment records, decommissioned passkeys, or proof of violations.

Over time, the line moved. Some buyers were genuine underground resistance.
Others were criminals, brokers, or factions willing to resell the data. His
leaks may have caused unintended harm: outages, ambushes, Kin hacks, lost jobs,
or instability in the same public systems he was supposed to protect. Once he
understood the damage, he tried to correct course by feeding selective
information to better allies, hiding keys, protecting communities, and building
a dead-man proof archive.

When CoreTech discovered what he was doing, they killed him and staged the death
as an accident. The official report blamed him through a liability loophole,
letting the company avoid compensation while discrediting him as careless or
responsible for his own death.

This loss is a core wound:

- grief
- anger at corpos
- distrust of official reports
- frustration with legal systems that protect money
- resentment toward public indifference
- pressure to become the provider too early

The father should not be reduced to a plot device. His death shaped the family,
but his life should later be remembered through habits, tools, sayings, old
repair knowledge, faith, and the protagonist's sense of duty.

The late-story reveal should reframe him without making him flawless. He was not
just a victim of CoreTech; he was quietly resisting it. He carried the moral
burden of working inside a harmful company because he believed his access could
help protect people and Kin from worse harm. He was also guilty of selling
secrets and making desperate choices before he fully understood who would use
the information and who might get hurt.

### Mother

The mother is alive and should be treated as an important emotional anchor. She
is part of why the protagonist works so hard. Details remain open, but she
should not be flattened into helplessness; she likely carries grief, faith,
practical wisdom, and daily survival pressure.

### Little Sister

The sister is around 5-8 years old.

She tries to help with small jobs:

- scrubbing dirty scrap for resale
- sorting harmless parts
- cleaning components
- handing tools
- doing little odds and ends around the repair space

She has a clear proclivity for tech and loves treating AI cores/Kin as friends.
Her warmth toward ARCs/Kin can help reveal the world before politics harden it:
to her, they are not assets or threats first. They are beings to talk to, clean,
name, comfort, and help.

She can become one of the emotional reasons the protagonist refuses to sell out,
even when selling rare tech would solve short-term money problems.

## Dream And Pressure

Like many people around him, the protagonist dreams of getting off the islands
and escaping the cycle of corruption, poverty, servitude, status pressure, and
limited opportunity.

That dream conflicts with his family obligations. Leaving could mean survival,
but staying means protecting his mother and sister. CoreLink should enter his
life at exactly this pressure point: a chance to sell something valuable, escape,
or use it to fight for something bigger.

## Long-Arc Goal

The protagonist's central long-term drive is to avenge his father, protect his
mother and little sister, and eventually get his family off the island.

The revenge arc should not be simple power fantasy. His father was killed by
corporate negligence and then erased through paperwork, liability games, and
public indifference. The protagonist begins wanting justice for one family, but
the deeper he goes, the more he sees that his father's death was part of a
larger system that destroys workers, exploits Kin, corrupts public projects, and
keeps poor families trapped.

**CoreTech** is the current working name for the primary corpo tied to the
father's death and the larger ARC/K.I.N. exploitation plot. It can be refined
later, but for now CoreTech is the name attached to the antagonist corporation
the protagonist eventually helps bring down.

Possible endgame direction:

1. The protagonist, his ArcKin, and allied factions expose and dismantle a major
   CoreTech operation.
2. His influence, proof, or leverage gives him a real chance to get his mother
   and sister safely off the island.
3. He realizes leaving everyone else behind would betray what he has learned.
   The island is still dangerous, the K.I.N. crisis is unresolved, ArcKin remain
   exploited, and ordinary people are still trapped.
4. He sends his family away, stays behind to help liberate remaining ArcKin and
   communities, and becomes a visible threat to surviving CoreTech loyalists,
   corpos, bounty hunters, and faction enemies.
5. Before parting, he gives his sister the Core Link, access to a meaningful
   Core Cache/Void Grid reserve, and the ARC from the family's outdated helper
   bot. He wipes sensitive history first, echoing the mystery of the previous
   owner and turning the handoff into a bridge between generations instead of a
   clean ending.

The ending should be bittersweet: he gets his family a way out, but he does not
get peace yet.

He chooses the sister because she has always shown a natural feel for Kin and
tech. Their mother is practical, faithful, and strong, but she does not fully
understand the systems inside the Core Link. The sister does. Even as a child,
she treats ARCs as beings, notices when they are distressed, and reaches for
technical answers faster than adults expect. The protagonist may not understand
her later resonance gift yet, but he knows the device and helper ARC will become
more useful in her hands as she grows.

The helper ARC matters emotionally. It is not just equipment. It is part of the
family's daily life, repair shop routine, and memory of home. Taking the ARC out
of the old helper body and sending it with the sister makes the goodbye more
personal: he is not only giving her a powerful tool, he is giving her a familiar
companion that can protect, guide, and remember.

## Family Helper ARC Story Thread

The family helper bot can become part of the main story before the ending
handoff.

Possible mid-game inheritance beat:

1. While infiltrating or moving through CoreTech, the protagonist finds an old
   worker locker that belonged to his father and somehow has not been fully
   repurposed.
2. Inside is a locked box, but it requires a passkey or identity key the
   protagonist does not have.
3. The box is immobile, secured, or too risky to remove, so he needs another way
   to open it.
4. He realizes someone at home may know something. His mother and sister are not
   available, but the family helper bot remains.
5. He asks the helper bot about the locker or box. The helper instructs him to
   bring its ARC to the lockbox.
6. He removes the ARC from the helper chassis, carries it back, and the ARC
   deciphers or authenticates the passkey.
7. The lockbox opens.

The contents should remain flexible for now. Possible directions:

- a birthday gift from the father, because he died near the protagonist's
  birthday
- a personal tool, repair module, or Core Link-compatible component
- a proof fragment about the accident
- a message or prayer recording
- a blueprint, passkey, or ARC-related object the father hid without fully
  understanding its later importance

This beat gives the helper ARC direct emotional weight. Once the protagonist
removes it from the family bot, circumstances can prevent him from returning it.
The story pulls him forward, and the helper ARC remains with him. At the end,
when he gives the Core Link to his sister, he also remembers that the helper ARC
belongs with the family and sends it with her.

## Sequel Seed

A second game can shift the playable role to the sister.

After the first game, the mother and sister board a ship meant to reach the
Americas or a safer off-island destination. A heavy storm strikes, the ship
crashes near another island, and they are cut off from normal communication.

The mother and sister survive without him through the mother's survival sense
and the sister's technical intuition. They hide from the storm, cling to each
other, find a small cave or shelter, and clumsily build a small fire. In fear
and frustration, the sister remembers the Core Link, stares at it, and finally
boots it. The device is wiped enough to protect everyone, but it still contains
emergency capability: the helper ARC, a usable Core Cache/Void Grid reserve, and
enough access to attempt a distress signal.

The sister figures out how to send an SOS. A ship answers, but it is not the
rescue she expects. She and her mother are captured by ex-CoreTech employees,
loyalists, sympathizers, or displaced operators who want leverage against the
new CoreTech leadership and anyone involved in the company's collapse.

This gives the second game or bridge story a clean motive:

- the sister inherits the Core Link myth without repeating the exact first boot
  story
- the family becomes active again rather than just rescued
- the first game's victory creates consequences
- CoreTech does not vanish cleanly; its remains become dangerous
- the sister can eventually grow from protected child into a protagonist with
  her own bond to ArcKin, faith, family, survival, and justice

Because the sister is only around 5-8 during the first game, she should not
become the immediate playable action lead unless meaningful time has passed. A
second game built around her should likely use a time skip, placing her in her
teens or older so she can make dangerous choices with agency. If the ship crash
and capture happen immediately after game one, that material may work better as
a bridge story, prologue, comic arc, or inciting incident that later shapes her
older playable arc.

The sister's later story should not only be "save the brother" or "repeat his
arc." She begins from the aftermath of his choices, carrying his wiped device,
family memory, and a different relationship with Kin: more trusting, more
emotionally open, and possibly more dangerous because of it.

## Story Function

The protagonist should let the audience experience Core Link through:

- practical repair work
- scavenger survival
- anger at corpo systems
- compassion toward overlooked people
- faith and moral pressure
- family responsibility
- curiosity about ARCs/Kin
- tension between escaping and staying

He should not start as a professional hacker, soldier, or elite battler. He is a
talented survivor with enough skill and heart to make the recovered CoreLink
matter.

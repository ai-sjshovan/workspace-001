# Core Link Core Tech And Bot Assembly

This document captures how AI cores, microfabricators, void storage, and chassis
assembly work. The goal is to ground the fantastical tech enough that it feels
like a system, not a plot hole.

## Core Principle

An AI core is not just a brain. It is a compact mind, secure memory vessel,
deployment seed, micro-workshop, and personal body vault.

Clean sentence:

> An AI core opens like a mechanical seed, releases microfabricators, accesses
> its Core Cache, projects a Matrix Scaffold, and assembles a body from stored
> scrap.

## AI Core Physical Design

AI cores are faceted geometric artifacts with an equator seam.

Important traits:

- hard outer shell
- flat luminous core faceplate
- visible equator seam
- shell splits open during deployment
- inner perforated fabricator tube
- internal microfabricator capsule slot
- personal Core Cache access
- display/status band or faceplate

When assembled into a bot, the core remains visibly present. Its faceplate should
be exposed somewhere on the chassis: chest, head, mask, shoulder, abdomen, or
central body. The light brightens/dims with voice and changes by mood/status.

Faceplate states:

- idle: soft cyan pulse
- speaking: waveform brightness
- low power: dim amber flicker
- angry/danger: sharp red scan bars
- curious/scan: violet rings
- damaged: broken light segments
- trusting/stable: slow clean glow

## Internal Slots

AI cores can have upgradeable internal systems.

### Microfabricator Capsule

A replaceable capsule injected into the core's center tube. When the core opens,
the tube releases the microfabricators.

Better capsules improve:

- assembly speed
- precision
- repair quality
- material compatibility
- Charge efficiency
- swarm density
- safe complexity limit

Illegal capsules may bypass safety limits, but should carry instability, trace,
or corruption risks.

### Void Storage License / Core Cache

AI cores are manufactured with personal void-storage access for body materials.
This storage is not mystical; it is licensed access to a real off-shore physical
matter storage network.

Example cache sizes:

- `8x8x8`
- `16x16x16`
- larger premium cubes later

The core accesses this via a Cache Key. The key grants network access to a
specific cube of stored matter.

### Material Loadout

The body is assembled from scrap/components assigned to the core's Core Cache.
Materials have:

- quality
- size
- weight
- compatibility
- profile availability
- durability
- conductivity or specialty traits later

Material occupies cache space. Better materials create better chassis results
but reduce available storage capacity.

### Items Later

Items can later include repair kits, move modules, firmware chips, energy tanks,
signal keys, tools, cache keys, and specialized chassis components.

## Deployment Ritual

Deployment should be iconic.

Sequence:

1. Core exits cradle or storage.
2. Shell seam glows.
3. Core splits open at the equator.
4. Inner fabricator tube releases microfabricators.
5. A small void aperture opens above/behind the core.
6. Stored scrap/components feed from Core Cache.
7. AI/Core Link projects Matrix Scaffold.
8. Microfabricators move between core, aperture, and scaffold.
9. Chassis forms around the core.
10. Faceplate lights and voice/mood initializes.

UI example:

```text
CORE DEPLOYMENT
shell: split
fabricator tube: open
capsule: MF-CIV/02
core cache: 8x8x8 active
material feed: scrap_mix_common
matrix scaffold: projected
assembly: started
```

## Matrix Scaffold

The AI core has body intent. Core Link and/or the core translates that intent
into a holographic assembly blueprint: the Matrix Scaffold.

The scaffold tells microfabricators where to place materials. It is the bridge
between identity and chassis.

The final body is shaped by:

- AI intent
- Matrix Scaffold translation
- microfabricator capsule quality
- material quality
- known material profiles
- available Charge
- time and safety limits

## Microfabricators

Use **microfabricators** as the preferred term. People may casually call them
nanobots, but the tech should be bounded.

Microfabricators are constrained fabrication tools. They are excellent at
cooperative assembly and poor at arbitrary hostile disassembly.

They require:

- Charge
- known material profiles
- permitted operations
- compatible inorganic material
- scaffold/chassis pattern
- control bandwidth
- authorization

They cannot normally:

- attack organic bodies
- consume buildings
- erase cities
- instantly destroy active defended bots
- create materials from nothing
- replicate freely

## Material Profiles

Microfabricators do not understand matter by default. They need material
profiles:

- composition
- stress behavior
- safe cut points
- assembly tolerances
- permitted operations
- authorization signature

Unsupported material should limit operations to scan or reject.

Example:

```text
MATERIAL PROFILE: scrap_aluminum_03
sync: 94%
operations: cut, fuse, reshape, plate
organic interaction: prohibited
```

## Medical And Military Variants

Medical microfabricators can exist, but they are a different regulated class:

- organic-safe
- operation-limited
- clinic-controlled
- biometric-authorized
- mostly deliver, seal, image, cauterize, or assist surgery

Military or illegal microfabricators can bypass safety limits, but they should
be rare, unstable, traceable, and story-level threats.

## Void Grid

The **Void Grid** is the corporate physical-matter storage network. It is
off-shore, automated, surveilled, and expensive.

Corporations sell:

- storage licenses
- cache keys
- larger cubes
- insurance/recovery
- reclaim services
- certified material storage

Hackers attack the Void Grid to steal access, squat storage, reroute ownership,
free locked materials, or obtain storage without paying.

This makes storage political: corporations charge rent for body material and
physical existence.

## Core Cradles

AI cores can be carried in safe housings on a belt, harness, case, or Core Link
carrier.

Possible terms:

- Core Cradle
- Link Cradle
- Core Clip
- Core Sheath

Default term: **Core Cradle**.

In the future 2D game, an operator can throw or release a cradled core. The core
opens in midair or on impact and assembles the chassis before/while landing.

## Reconfiguration And Mimic Cores

Some rare AI cores like to mimic what they see. They understand by becoming.

They can observe:

- motion
- silhouette
- armor profile
- weapon pattern
- posture
- behavior
- utility layout

Then they generate a new Matrix Scaffold inspired by that observation.

Advanced mimic/reconfiguration cores can change chassis often, even mid-battle.
This gives strategic flexibility but costs resources.

Costs and limits:

- consumes Charge
- increases heat/stability strain
- may expose the core
- requires compatible stored materials
- requires high-grade microfabricators
- depends on scanned/learned profiles
- specialists remain stronger at their specialty

Example:

```text
MIMIC FRAME SHIFT
profile: BULWARK SHELL
cost: 18 Charge
risk: +12 heat
core exposure: 1 turn
```

Design rule:

> Specialists are efficient. Mimic cores are unpredictable.
